IVF Success Rates by State: CDC Data Compared

Median IVF success rates by state for patients under 35 and over 40, plus where most US IVF cycles happen, from CDC clinic data, with the limits of comparing states.

People often ask which state has the best IVF success rates. The CDC publishes outcomes for each reporting clinic, so we can summarize them by state. This guide shows the median clinic success rate in each state, by age group, and where most IVF cycles take place. It also explains why a state’s figure says little about any one clinic, or about your own chances.

All figures are from the CDC’s 2022 clinic data, the latest final year available.[1] The measure used is live births per intended egg retrieval for patients using their own eggs. It counts every cycle started with the intention of retrieving eggs, including cycles that were cancelled, and asks how many led to a live birth. Our guide on how to read IVF success rates explains this and the other measures the CDC reports.

How we calculated state figures

For each state, we took every clinic that reported 2022 outcomes to the CDC and found the median of their success rates for a given age group. The median is the middle clinic. Half the state’s clinics are above it and half below. We used the median, not an average weighted by patients, so that one very large clinic does not define a whole state.

We only rank states where at least five clinics reported a rate for that age group. With fewer clinics, the “state figure” is really just one or two clinics’ results. That would mislead more than it informs. In 2022, 51 states and districts had at least one reporting clinic. 24 of them had fewer than five.[1]

Success rates by state: patients under 35

The national figure for patients under 35 was 49.7% live births per intended retrieval.[1] Across the 26 states we can rank, the median clinic ranged from 41.9% to 56.6%, a spread of about 14.7 percentage points. In 13 of them, the median clinic was at or above the national figure.[1]

Why state comparisons have limits

The CDC cautions that comparing clinics may not be meaningful. Clinics treat different patient populations, and their success rates depend on factors such as patients’ ages, diagnoses and how many cycles they have had.[3] Comparing states adds more layers to the same problem:

  • Patient mix. States where more patients start IVF later, or after other treatments have not worked, will tend to show lower rates for the same age group.
  • Clinic mix. Some states have a few very large clinics, others many small ones. Smaller clinics’ rates vary more from year to year.
  • Practice patterns. Clinics differ in how often they freeze all embryos, use genetic testing, or bank eggs for later. Those choices affect when, and from which transfer, a birth is counted.
  • Travel. Patients cross state lines for treatment, so a state’s figures are not a picture of its residents.

For these reasons, a state’s median says most about the typical clinic there. It does not tell you how a specific clinic, or your own treatment, will go.

How to use this information

  1. Start with your state’s page. Each state page lists every reporting clinic with its rates by age, volume and the services it reported to the CDC,[2] and you can sort and filter.
  2. Compare clinics within your age group. Look at the row for your age, and check the number of retrievals behind each rate. Rates based on few cycles are less stable.
  3. Look beyond one number. Our FertilityList Score combines age-adjusted outcomes with volume, reporting consistency and lab standards.
  4. Ask clinics directly. Use our questions to ask a fertility clinic.

Questions to ask

  • How do your success rates for my age group compare with the national figure, and how many cycles are they based on?
  • What share of your patients are over 40, or have had treatment elsewhere before?
  • Do you freeze all embryos or use genetic testing routinely, and how does that affect your reported rates?
  • If I travel from another state, what does monitoring look like, and how many visits are needed in person?

Bottom line

Success rates differ by state, but the differences are smaller than the differences between age groups. They also partly reflect who each state’s clinics treat.[1][3] Use state figures as context, then compare individual clinics on your state’s page.
