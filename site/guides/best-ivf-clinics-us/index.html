“Which IVF clinic is the best?” has no single answer. It depends on your age, diagnosis, budget and where you live. What the CDC’s clinic data can show is which clinics reported consistently strong outcomes for their patients’ ages, at meaningful volume. This guide ranks US clinics on that basis using the FertilityList Score, built only from public CDC data for 2022.[1][2]

How the ranking works

The FertilityList Score runs from 1.0 to 5.0. It combines four components:[1][2]

  • Age-adjusted outcomes (50% of the score). For each age group, we compare the clinic’s live births per intended egg retrieval with the national rate. Each age group is weighted by how many of the clinic’s patients were that age. A clinic that treats many patients over 40 is not penalized for their lower success rates.[1]
  • Experience (20%). The number of ART cycles the clinic reported, on a logarithmic scale, so going from 50 to 500 cycles counts much more than going from 2,000 to 2,450.[1]
  • Reporting consistency (15%). How many of the last three years the clinic reported to the CDC.[1]
  • Professional standards (15%). Whether the clinic reported SART membership and lab accreditation in the CDC’s services data.[2]

Clinics with fewer than 20 intended retrievals in the latest year are not scored, because a handful of patients can swing their rates. In 2022, 390 reporting clinics received a score and 62 did not.[1] Full details, including the small-sample adjustment, are on our methodology page.

What this ranking cannot tell you

The CDC cautions that comparing clinics’ success rates may not be meaningful. Clinics treat different patient populations, and their rates depend on factors such as patient age, diagnosis and treatment history.[3] Our age adjustment addresses the largest of these factors, but not all of them:

  • Diagnosis mix. A clinic that treats many patients after failed treatment elsewhere may report lower rates for the same age group.
  • Practice patterns. Clinics differ in how often they freeze all embryos, bank eggs or use genetic testing. That changes when, and from which transfer, a birth is counted.
  • What is not in the data. The CDC does not report prices, wait times, communication, or how patients feel about their care.[1]
  • One year. Scores use the latest final year, 2022. Each clinic page shows up to three years so you can check whether results are consistent.

For these reasons, treat the ranking as a shortlist to investigate, not a verdict. The same data can be explored by size in the largest IVF clinics in the US and by geography in IVF success rates by state.

How to use this list

  1. Start local. Open your state’s page and sort by score or by the success rate for your age group.
  2. Check your age row. On each clinic page, compare the rate for your age with the national figure, and look at how many retrievals it is based on.
  3. Look at services. If you need donor eggs, a gestational carrier or egg freezing, confirm the clinic reported offering them.[2]
  4. Ask questions. Use our questions to ask a fertility clinic at consultations, and see SART membership and lab accreditation for what those standards mean.

Bottom line

The highest-scoring US clinics combine strong age-adjusted CDC outcomes with high volume, consistent reporting and reported professional standards.[1][2] Rankings are a starting point. The best clinic for you is one with good results for patients like you, the services you need, and a team you trust.
